Design proposal · 3 August 2026 · branch phase-1

Nav map, Waze treatment

Five changes to the practice-drive map: a coloured base with fat roads, a puck built like Waze’s, GPS snapped onto the road, and a camera that stops twitching. Route line and puck arrow stay emerald — the layout is Waze, the identity is yours.

Map base

Colour and road width

Today the map is light-v11 — near-white, thin roads, nothing to read at a glance. Waze’s map is legible at speed because roads are wide and white against a warm off-white land, with parks, water and motorways carrying the only other colour.

Now — light-v11

Roads read as faint scratches. The route is the only object with weight, so junctions ahead are invisible.

Proposed

Roads carry the map; the emerald route sits on top of them rather than being the map. Junction shape is readable three blocks ahead.

How: switch the base style to mapbox://styles/mapbox/navigation-day-v1, which already ships wide roads and nav colouring, then override the handful of land, water and road-width layers at runtime using rnmapbox’s existing layer prop.

Risk, and the fallback. If overriding existing layers turns out to be unreliable in Expo Go, I’ll author the same palette as a custom style in Mapbox Studio and point NAV_STYLE at that URL instead. Identical result, one line of app code, but it needs your Mapbox account.

User marker

The puck

Waze’s puck is three stacked layers, not one icon: a soft grey pad on the road that gives the car physical presence, a white disc, and a solid arrow. Today we render a rotated Ionicons paper-plane inside an emerald circle, which reads as a pin, not a vehicle.

Now

Solid emerald disc, white ring, glyph rotated −45° as a hack. Heavy, and it hides the road under it.

Proposed

Grey presence pad + white disc + emerald arrow with a darker outline. Sits on the road, and the road still shows through.

Waze, for reference

Same three layers. Only the arrow fill differs — that’s the one place we stay emerald.

  • The arrow is a real SVG chevron pointing straight up, replacing the rotate(-45deg) Ionicons workaround.
  • While the camera is following, up is your heading, so the arrow needs no rotation at all — it only rotates once you pan away from follow mode.
  • The puck moves from screen centre to roughly 62% down the map via camera padding, so you see more road ahead. That is why Waze feels like it’s looking forward.

Positioning

Snap to the road

Raw GPS wanders 5–15 m, so the puck drifts through gardens and jumps between lanes. Because we already hold the full route polyline, we can project each fix onto it — no extra API, no network, works offline.

  • Two new pure functions in src/lib/geo.ts: projectOnSegment() and snapToPath(). Both unit-tested before the screen touches them.
  • Only segments within a ±30-vertex window around the current trail index are searched, so a loop route never snaps you onto the return leg.
  • If the perpendicular distance exceeds 40 m, we assume you’ve genuinely left the route and fall back to raw GPS. That threshold is also the natural hook for rerouting later.
  • The snap gives us the road bearing for free. Above 3 mph the puck points down the road instead of wherever the compass thinks the phone is facing — this alone removes most of the wobble.

Camera

Less sensitive

Three separate things cause the twitch: unfiltered compass, hard zoom thresholds that flip back and forth while you hover near them, and lateral GPS noise. Each gets its own fix, and none of them slow down how quickly a real turn registers.

KnobNowProposedWhy
Puck heading source raw compass, every fix road bearing > 3 mph Compass swings with hand movement; the road doesn’t.
Heading filter none circular EMA α=0.15 Smooths jitter while still turning a full 90° in about a second.
Heading deadband 0° 3° Sub-3° wiggle never reaches the camera at all.
Camera update rate every fix (~2/s) throttled to 4/s max Caps how often the native camera is asked to re-aim.
Zoom levels 19 / 18 / 17.5 18.6 / 18.0 / 17.5 Smaller steps make a change barely perceptible.
Zoom thresholds <150 m, >600 m hysteresis 130/180 and 550/650 Stops the flip-flop when you sit right on a boundary.
Puck position raw GPS snapped to route Kills sideways drift outright.
GPS rate 500 ms / 1 m unchanged Responsiveness is kept; the noise is filtered downstream, not sampled away.

What stays fast. Voice prompts, distance-to-manoeuvre and step advancement all keep running on the unfiltered fix at full rate. Only what you see move is smoothed.

Not in this piece of work

  • Rerouting when you leave the route — the 40 m off-route signal is added, but nothing acts on it yet.
  • The top instruction banner, speedometer and bottom bar keep their current design.
  • Traffic, reports, and anything else Waze-specific beyond the map surface.
  • Dark / night map. Worth doing, but it doubles the palette work — separate job.
