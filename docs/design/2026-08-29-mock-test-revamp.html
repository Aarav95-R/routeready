RouteReady · design · 29 August 2026

Revamping the mock test

app/route/[id]/mock.tsx is the one screen in the app operated by somebody other than the learner — a parent or instructor in the passenger seat, marking faults on a moving car’s dashboard. It is also the oldest screen still on the pre-restage type and the cool Tailwind greys. Five decisions below, each with the options side by side. Nothing is built yet.

The through-line

Every one of those is the same problem: the marking surface and the driving surface are fighting for one screen, and the fight has been settled with pixel constants. The five decisions below try to settle it with structure instead.

05 · decision four

The tally becomes headroom

src/lib/scoring/engine.ts is unambiguous: passed = majors === 0 && minors <= 15. The screen already knows that during the drive — and reports 3 min   0 maj   +1 in monospace instead.

Recommended A · Fifteen ticks

12 driving faults left +1 credit
No serious faults
One serious fault — a real test would end here
3 driving faults left +1 credit

Fifteen ticks, one per allowed driving fault, filled as they land and turning amber past the tenth. A serious fault is not a sixteenth tick — it is a different outcome, so it takes its own bar and the strip goes red behind it. Readable in the quarter-second a passenger can spare.

Alternative B · One number

12 faults
left
0 serious
+1 credit

The number alone, at 34 pt — the largest thing on the screen after the map. Nothing to decode. Loses the sense of proportion a strip gives you: 12 left and 3 left look identical until you read the digits, and reading is the thing we are trying to avoid.

One honesty problem to settle before this ships

A meter that says “12 left” is claiming to know the verdict. Right now it can’t: aggregate() also counts speeding runs and missed observation zones, and those are only computed at finish(). Missed zones are actually known live — zoneOutcomes is written as each zone closes — so the gap is speeding alone. Either we run the speeding detector incrementally over samples so the meter is true, or the meter is labelled “marked faults” and stops implying a verdict. My preference is the first; the second is a one-line fallback if it turns out to be expensive on-device.

06 · decision five

Mirrors, Finish, and the red that never leaves

Three controls float in a row 150 pt off the bottom, hand-placed to miss the puck by half a point. Two of them are pressed once a drive; one is pressed at every observation zone, on a deadline.

  • Mirrors keeps 74 pt and gains a ring that drains while the zone is live, so the passenger can see how long they have rather than guessing from a glow.
  • Idle is white-on-map, not slate-300 — a real control that is currently off, rather than a dead grey disc. Active stays #1A875E.
  • Finish test leaves the row. It is pressed once, at the end, and it currently sits beside the two controls pressed while moving. It belongs in the sheet, under the meter, with the confirm dialog it already has.
  • Exit loses its red. A quiet outlined pill in the status bar, same confirm behind it. That frees #DC2626 to mean exactly one thing on this screen: a serious fault.
  • The row is stacked off the sheet’s height the way the Mapbox logo already is, rather than a tuned literal. That deletes the 0.5 pt-clearance comment and the class of bug it documents.

Consider Mirrors as the whole bar

When a zone is live, a full-width green bar appears where nothing was before — ~294×60 pt, impossible to miss or mis-hit — and vanishes when the zone closes. No permanent grey disc taking up the bottom of the map for the 38 minutes it isn’t needed.

Cost: a control that appears and disappears is a control the passenger has to re-find each time, and its arrival is itself a prompt — which arguably makes the mirror check too easy to pass.

07 · underneath all five

The restage

This screen predates the type ladder and the warm-neutral palette. Whichever options above are picked, these swaps happen anyway — they are what makes it sit beside Quiz and Manoeuvres rather than looking like a different app.

WhatTodayBecomesWhy
Font sizes 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 18 17 / 15 / 13 — and 34 if a number leads Seven sizes, none of them on the ladder in src/ui/type.ts. Four of the seven are below 13 pt on a screen read at arm’s length in a car.

Fault levels #FEF3C7 / #FEE2E2 / #ECFDF5 Unchanged These are already right and already centralised in LEVEL_STYLE. The amber and red carry meaning; leave them alone.
Two palette rules this design holds to

No second green. The route line is #1A875E and so is Mirrors-when-live, because both mean “go, now”. The headroom meter therefore never uses green for “you’re fine” — unfilled ticks are fill, and the strip only takes colour as it fills. Nothing on this screen competes with the road for a green glance.

No clay. #B8624A owns progress and the person’s own account, and it appears nowhere on a live drive. The meter is amber-to-red, not clay, even though it is technically progress.

08 · boundary

What none of this touches

  • The nav loop — onPosition, navFix, navSnap, the compass smoothing and the camera pushes. Untouched.
  • routeLifecycleReducer and the preview → approach → at-start → test → finish-prompt legs. This redesign only re-dresses the test leg; the other four keep their current cards.
  • Scoring. aggregate() stays a pure function of the faults handed to it. The only open question is whether the speeding detector also runs incrementally so the live meter can be honest.
  • Off-route detection, the return leg, and deviation recording. Only where the banner is drawn changes.
  • The result screen. It has its own restage due, separately.
