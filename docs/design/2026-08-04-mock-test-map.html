Same map, different job

The mock test is further behind than it looks. It is not a version of the practice map with fewer features — it is still on a different map engine entirely. And it is the one screen where the map feeds a score, which is where the one genuine risk sits.

Structure

Extract the map, keep the screens

The two screens want an identical map and completely different chrome. That is exactly the split.

Goes into the shared map

src/ui/NavMap.tsx

The Mapbox view, the ~60 base-layer overrides, manual camera with follow and recenter, snap to road, the rotation model, the route line with its progress split, the puck, and start/finish markers. One component, one place to fix a map bug.

Stays in each screen

practice.tsx · mock.tsx

Everything that is not the map. Practice keeps its instruction banner, voice prompts, step advancement and phase machine. Mock keeps its timer, observation zones, mirrors button, scoring samples and finish flow. Neither screen knows how the other works.

This is the extraction the last code review already asked for, and a second consumer is what settles the shape of it — designing a shared component against one caller is guesswork.

The one real decision

Does the score see snapped positions, or raw?

The mock test is the only screen where the map feeds something that judges the driver. Snapping moves the reported position onto the route line — which is exactly what makes the puck stop drifting through gardens, and exactly what could put a driver inside an observation zone they never entered.

Option A · recommended

Score on raw, draw on snapped

The map shows the tidy snapped position; onSample keeps receiving the untouched GPS fix it gets today. Observation zones and speeding faults are judged on what the phone actually measured, so the display can never invent a fault or hide one.

It also means this change cannot alter anyone's score, which makes it safe to ship without re-validating the scoring engine.

Option B

Score on snapped too

More stable zone detection: GPS noise can currently flick a driver in and out of a zone boundary. But snapping assumes you are on the route, and a mock test is precisely where someone might not be — take a wrong turn and the snap keeps reporting you on the line, potentially crediting a zone you drove nowhere near.

Separately, and worth saying plainly: the practice screen still has not been confirmed working on your phone. Extracting its map into a shared component means that if something turns out to be broken, it will be harder to tell whether it was broken before the extraction or by it. Driving the practice route once first would remove that ambiguity entirely. Your call — I can also just build it and you check both together.

Result

What the mock test looks like

Same map as practice, with the mock test's own chrome on top: the running clock, the observation zone prompt, the mirrors button and finish.

  • Camera follows heading-up and pitched, exactly as practice does. There are no turn instructions in a mock test, so the zoom sits at one fixed navigation level instead of tightening into manoeuvres.
  • The route shows progress: grey behind, emerald ahead. Consistent with practice, and it doubles as a read on how far through the test you are.
  • Observation zones become visible on the map rather than only announced by a toast. They are the thing being scored; drawing them costs nothing now that the map can.
  • The mirrors button moves down slightly. The puck sits at 62% of screen height and the button is currently centred near there — on the pitched map they would overlap.
