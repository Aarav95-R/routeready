Design check · 3 August 2026

Green ahead, grey behind

The route line should show progress: bright emerald for the road still to drive, grey for the part already done. Today both halves are the same emerald, so there is nothing to read.

The route

What changes

The line is already split in two at your position — a “travelled” piece and an “ahead” piece, updated on every fix. They are just both painted emerald. Only the travelled half’s two colours change.

Now

One unbroken emerald ribbon. You cannot tell at a glance how far through you are.

Proposed

Progress reads instantly, and the only bright thing on the map is the road you still have to drive.

Ahead, core#059669 · unchanged
Ahead, casing#04684C · unchanged
Driven, core#A7AEA5 · new
Driven, casing#7F8880 · new

The greys carry a slight green bias so they belong to the same map as the road casings (#C3C8C1) rather than looking like a different app’s furniture. Both are darker than the road casing on purpose — a driven route still has to read as a line where it runs along a white road, not disappear into it.

The driven half also thins slightly, from 15/10 px to 14/9. It already sits a shade under the road ahead; taking one more pixel off makes “done” recede without any extra colour work.

One decision

What about the drive to the start?

“Guide me to the start” draws a second, separate line: the way from wherever you are to the beginning of the test route. That leg is already grey on purpose, to say “this is not the test yet”. With grey about to mean “driven”, it now says two things at once.

Option A · recommended

Same rule everywhere

Green always means “road you still have to drive”, grey always means “done”. One rule, no exceptions to remember. You lose the visual cue that separates the approach from the test route — but the banner already says Heading to the start in green the whole time.

Option B

Approach stays fully grey

Keeps today’s behaviour: the whole approach leg is muted, so emerald is reserved for the actual test route and appears the moment it begins. The cost is that grey now means two different things, and the road ahead of you on the approach looks the same as the road behind.

Scope

The work

  • Two new colour constants in src/ui/mapStyle.ts, alongside the existing route pair, so every route colour lives in one place.
  • The travelled line’s two layers in practice.tsx point at them instead of the emerald pair, and drop a pixel of width.
  • A test pinning the exact values, matching how the rest of the palette is guarded.
  • Whatever Option A or B implies for the approach leg.

No new logic: the split point already exists and already updates on every fix. Nothing here needs a rebuild.
