Two rotations, two sources

What I understood from “the icon should rotate when I turn the phone, but the map should only rotate when I’m turning around the road”. Read the four scenarios — if any of them is wrong, say which.

The model

What drives what

These are independent. Right now the app has only one rotation, the map’s, and the icon is nailed to straight-up — which is why nothing moves when you twist the phone.

Map bearing Rotates only when you travel in a new direction Comes from where the car is going: the bearing of the road segment you’re snapped to. Turn a corner and the map swings round. Sit at a red light twisting the phone and it does not move.
Icon rotation Rotates whenever the phone physically turns Comes from the compass. Because the icon sits on an already-rotated map, its angle is the compass heading minus the map bearing — the difference between where the phone points and where the road goes.
// map bearing — travel only, never the compass mapBearing = snapped road bearing when moving ≥ 3 mph and on the route = GPS course when moving but off the route = unchanged when stopped — this is the key line // icon rotation — compass, expressed relative to the rotated map iconRotation = smoothedCompass − mapBearing

The nice property of that subtraction: with the phone mounted facing forward, compass and map bearing are the same number, so the icon lands at 0° and points straight up on its own. No special case for the normal driving position.

Diagnosis

Why nothing turns today

The compass is running. Its readings just have nowhere to go.

Stand still and turn the phone and every link holds except the ones that matter: the compass updates a value nobody reads, and the icon is pinned. Before this, the map was rotated by Mapbox’s own FollowWithHeading mode, which consumed the compass natively. Taking manual control of the camera orphaned it, and no one caught it because none of this was ever run on a phone until you did.

The work

What I’d change

  • Icon rotation becomes live. The compass reading, smoothed, minus the current map bearing, held in React state so the icon actually re-renders. Throttled to about 20 a second — the compass fires far faster than that and the arrow only needs to look continuous.
  • The map stops listening to the compass. Today, below 3 mph the camera falls back to the raw compass. Under your rule that is wrong: stopped means the map holds still. Map bearing comes from travel only.
  • One seed at the start. On the first fix of a leg the map takes its bearing from the compass once, so you begin facing the way the car faces rather than north. After that, travel only.
  • Both rotations get their own smoothing. The map keeps the existing gentle ease. The arrow needs to feel immediate when you turn the phone in your hand, so it gets a lighter filter and a much shorter animation.
  • Bridge and tunnel layers. Separate from the rotation work: 32 unstyled layers, which is why roads change colour and width mid-flyover. They get the same palette and width tables as the roads they continue.

No rebuild needed. All of this is JavaScript, so it reaches your phone over live reload the same way the rest did.
