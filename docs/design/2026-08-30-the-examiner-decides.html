The examiner decides

Two corrections. There is no way to begin a test once you have been guided to the start — I checked, and you are right. And the score should come from the mark sheet alone: what the GPS thinks it saw is not evidence, and right now it can fail a drive the examiner passed.

Why the hand-over may never fire

Reaching the arrival prompt needs the lifecycle to see three accepted fixes, spanning three seconds, within 30 m of the start, under 5 mph. Two things make that fragile at exactly the moment it matters:

The location watch runs with distanceInterval: 5 — iOS delivers a fix only after five metres of movement. A car that has stopped emits nothing at all. So pulling up on the line and waiting is the one thing guaranteed not to produce the fixes the hand-over needs.

And 30 m is tight. Park on the other side of the road, or a few car lengths short, and the fixes are never eligible in the first place.

Either way there is no button anywhere on the approach screen, so the only way out is Exit — which abandons the drive.

Recommended A · A Begin bar on the approach

Heading to the start

Always live, from the moment the approach opens. It is the same beginTest() the preview card's Start calls, with the same two checks: a fix no older than five seconds, taken within 45 m of the start. Arrive early, park awkwardly, or stop and wait — there is always something to press.

The automatic arrival prompt stays exactly as it is. When it does fire it is still the nicer moment: it speaks, it vibrates, and it names the route. This is the floor under it, not a replacement.

Alternative B · Make the hand-over easier to hit

Leave the screen alone and loosen the rule: accept the arrival at 45 m rather than 30, and drop the three-fix span to one good fix.

It does not fix the real problem. A stopped car produces no fixes at all, so no threshold reaches the driver who has already parked on the line and is waiting to be let in. Loosening also makes the prompt more likely to fire while still rolling past the start, which is the case the three-fix span exists to prevent. Worth doing as well as A, perhaps; not instead of it.

02 · decision two

The score is the mark sheet, and nothing else

A real test is one examiner's judgement written on a DL25. This one currently blends that with two things a phone guessed.

Counted todayWhere it comes fromBecomes
Examiner marks The mark sheet. A person watched and pressed. The whole score
Speeding detectSpeedingFaults — GPS speed against a stored limit for the nearest route vertex. Over the limit for 5 s is scored a serious fault, which fails the test outright. Removed
Missed observation detectObservationFaults — a driving fault for every zone the car passed through without Mirrors being tapped. Removed
Why this is a correctness fix, not just a simplification

GPS speed on a phone is noisy, and the limit it is compared against is whatever speed_profile was seeded with for the nearest point on the route. A wrong limit segment, a tunnel, a moment of drift — and the drive is failed by a serious fault the examiner never saw and cannot undo, because auto-detected faults never appear on the sheet.

The same is true in reverse for observation: the driver checks their mirrors properly, the passenger does not happen to press a button, and the result records a fault against a driver who did nothing wrong.

Recommended A · Mirrors goes, the zones stay drawn

The Mirrors button exists to feed detectObservationFaults. With that gone it records nothing, and it is the largest control on the map. Remove it, and the zone prompt with it. Keep the zones drawn on the route: they are still the places where observation is being looked for, which is useful to see coming.

The examiner marks “Use of mirrors” on the sheet, like every other fault. One way to record a thing instead of two.

It also gives the bottom of the map back. The controls row currently clears the driver puck by −0.5 pt on an iPhone SE; without the 74 pt Mirrors button that pressure disappears.

Alternative B · Keep Mirrors as a prompt

The zone still lights the button and asks for a mirror check, and tapping it still feels like doing something — it just no longer scores. Useful as a coaching cue for the learner.

A control that changes nothing is a control that teaches people their presses do not matter, and this one sits in the most valuable spot on the screen. If the check is worth recording, the examiner should mark it.

Sub-decision What stops an empty test scoring 100?

With “not assessed” gone, opening a mock test and immediately finishing gives a clean sheet: pass, 100. Two ways to feel about it:

i. Leave it. A mock test is a tool someone chooses to use; a sheet with nothing on it means nobody was watching, and the person who did that knows.

ii. Guard on duration — a test under, say, five minutes is not offered a verdict. Honest, and it does not depend on GPS.

detectSpeedingFaults and detectObservationFaults stay in engine.ts with their tests. They are correct functions that nothing calls any more, and deleting them is a separate decision from deciding they should not score a mock test.

The one thing genuinely lost

Nothing catches a fault the examiner misses. Today, a sustained speed over the limit is recorded whether or not anyone noticed. After this, if the person in the passenger seat does not see it, it did not happen.

Which is exactly how a real test works, and is the point of the change — but it is worth saying out loud, because a mock test will now score higher than it used to for the same drive.

04 · boundary

What this does not touch

  • The mark sheet. Twenty-three categories, minus and plus per row, fifteen driving faults and one serious — all unchanged.
  • The map, the route line, off-route detection, the return leg, and deviation recording. Leaving the route is still not a fault.
  • The practice screen. It has its own store and never used any of this.
  • The pass rule itself: majors === 0 && minors <= 15.
  • GPS keeps drawing the map and driving the navigation. It stops being evidence; it does not stop being useful.
