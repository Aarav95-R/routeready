Starting a Round

The Quiz tab shipped with an entry point that never says what it starts. Three ways to fix that, at the width they will actually be read at.

What the card says now

A number, a bar, and a button. Everything a learner would want to know before tapping — that it is multiple choice, that it is ten questions, that it will not take long — is missing.

Three things it fails to say

  • “A round” of what. The word carries no meaning until you have already tapped it once. It is the only unexplained noun on the screen.
  • That it is multiple choice. Nothing anywhere on the tab says so. A learner who reads the rows below reasonably expects to be asked to recite.
  • How big a commitment it is. Ten questions is about two minutes. Not saying so makes it feel like an open-ended thing to start on a bus.

And one thing the whole screen fails to say

That the card and the list are two different tools — one to drill with, one to read. Right now the list just begins, with no line telling you what it is.

Built: A, with the button given more presence

Shipped as drawn, with two changes made during the build.

  • The button is 52pt, not 46, and carries a play glyph. The card explains clearly enough at 46, but the ask was for the start to be unmissable, and height plus an icon is where that comes from without shouting in colour.
  • The label is “Start the quiz”, not “Start a round”. “Round” is a word only this app uses. It stays in the summary, where you have already done one and it means something. Deliberately not “start the test”: in this app “test” is the thing at the test centre, and one noun should not mean both.

Why A over the other two

B and C both try to make one control carry the whole explanation, and a control is the wrong place for three facts. B’s button is the clearest single line of the three, but “Start a 10-question quiz” already wraps on a smaller phone and will wrap harder for anyone using larger text — and this app caps display type at 110% precisely because that setting gets used. C is the most iOS-literate answer and the wrong one: this is the tab’s primary action, and a chevron row is what you reach for when something is secondary.

A separates the two jobs. The sentence explains, the button acts, and neither has to compromise. It also has room to say the thing the other two cannot fit — that the words to say out loud come after each question — which is the actual reason to use the drill rather than just reading the list.

Text got darker across the whole app at the same time

Checked while making the card clearer: labelTertiary was #8A827A, which measures 3.78 against a cell and 3.33 against the field — under the 4.5 WCAG asks for at these sizes. It carries the second line of every row, every group header and every caption, so most of the app’s small type was below the threshold. Tertiary is now #6B635B (5.90 / 5.20) and secondary moved down to #574F49 (8.02 / 7.07) to keep three distinct levels. Both values were already in the palette. This changes every screen, not just this one, and theme.test.ts now holds the floor.

One change beyond the card, in all three

The list gains a lead-in header — All 20 questions — above the first category. Without it the tab reads as one feature that happens to have a button on top. With it, the split the design was built around is finally stated on screen: the card is the drill, the list is the reference.
