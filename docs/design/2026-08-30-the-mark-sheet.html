RouteReady · design · 30 August 2026

The mark sheet

Back to a listed sheet: every fault the examiner can give, on screen, with a minus and a plus on each row. Nothing to drill into, nothing to hunt for. This replaces the tile-and-severity flow I proposed last time — that made the common case fast and the sheet itself worse, which is the wrong way round for the person holding the phone for forty minutes.

A control per row. No mode, no selection, no second screen. The mark is made where the fault is named.

Three 12 pt lines per row. Two of them are worked examples nobody reads at 30 mph. They make the row 90 pt tall, so only three rows fit on screen at once — on a list of twenty-three.

Three equal buttons. A serious fault ends the test and a credit is worth a few points, yet both are the same size as the fault you give ten of.

Why the count sits between the two buttons

It is the stepper shape everyone already knows, and it puts the feedback exactly where the finger is. Press minus, the number under your thumb goes up and the meter at the top drops by one. You never have to look anywhere else to know the press registered — which is the whole problem with the row it replaces.

03 · decision one

What the plus does

“Plus or minus” reads two ways and they score differently, so this is the one thing I do not want to guess at.

Recommended A · Plus is a credit

Use of mirrors
S−3+
Move off✓ 1 done well
S−0+

Minus takes a fault, plus gives a credit — the same pair the original sheet had as MIN and +. Credits are already in the scoring engine: worth up to 10 points of encouragement on the score and nothing at all on the pass/fail verdict, which is deliberate.

Correcting a mis-tap uses Undo at the top, which removes the last mark whatever it was. That already exists and already works.

Alternative B · Plus takes one back

Use of mirrors
S−3+

A true stepper: minus adds a fault to this row, plus removes one. Every row corrects itself, so a mis-tap never needs the Undo at the top and never needs to be noticed within a few seconds.

Costs the credit. There would be nowhere left to say “they did that one well”, so creditCount() and the up-to-10-point bonus would go unused, and the result screen loses the only positive thing it currently says. Choose this if credits are not something you want in a mock test.

04 · decision two

What happened to the examples

Every category carries a written example of what makes it a driving fault and what makes it serious. They are good, and they are the reason only three rows fit on screen.

Tapping the name opens that row's two examples; the buttons keep working while it is open. Rows stay 66 pt, the whole sheet stays scannable, and the guidance is one tap away at a red light rather than three lines of noise at 30 mph.

The row is the tap target for the examples, so nothing on it does two jobs: name opens, minus marks, plus credits.

The driving-fault example stays on the row permanently, at a readable 13 pt rather than 12. Rows are 78 pt, so seven fit instead of nine. The serious example is only in the expanded view.

Better for somebody marking their first test; slower to scan by the third.

The score leads. Headroom in the largest type on the sheet, the fifteen ticks under it, and the detail line below that. It is pinned, so it never scrolls away from the row being marked.

2

Undo is in the header, reachable from any scroll position. It removes the last mark whatever it was — a fault, a serious, a credit.

3

A marked row tints. A faint amber wash, so the rows that carry the test's story are findable by eye when scrolling back.

4

Group headers in the type ladder's own footnote size rather than monospace, so the sheet stops looking like a debug panel.

5

The clock moves here. It is the examiner's number, not the driver's, and the map's status bar keeps its own copy.

6

Nine rows visible, so “While driving” — sixteen of the twenty-three, and where nearly every mark lands — is two flicks rather than six.

Where it sits when it is not being used

Two resting heights: the score alone (about 105 pt, the map keeps the road, the puck, Mirrors and the Mapbox attribution) and full. Marking is one drag from the first — and the drag can start anywhere on the sheet, not just on the handle. There is no third middle height in this design: the whole point of a listed sheet is that it wants the screen.

One honest gap: at 48 pt the minus and plus are 8 pt apart, which is fine for a passenger and tight for anyone with a tremor. If that matters, the S moves into the expanded row and the two main buttons grow to 56 pt — say so and I will draw it that way instead.

07 · boundary

What this does not change

  • The categories themselves. Twenty-three, in DVSA's own words off the DL25, in three groups. Untouched.
  • Scoring. Fifteen driving faults allowed, one serious fails, credits worth up to ten points and nothing on the verdict.
  • The live meter, which keeps counting what the verdict counts — marks, missed observation zones and speeding.
  • The map, Mirrors, the message slot, off-route, finish. The sheet is what changes.
  • The eight-tile grid and the severity step go away entirely. They were the wrong shape for this job.
