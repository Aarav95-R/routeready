Design check · 4 August 2026

An examiner in the passenger seat

Two changes to the mock test: the map becomes the practice map, and whoever is sitting beside the learner gets a panel of the things a real examiner marks — tapped down for a fault, up for something done well, feeding the result at the end.

The panel

Closed by default, one pull away

A sheet over the bottom of the map. Closed it is a single bar showing the running tally, so the map stays readable and nothing is in the way. Pulled up it covers the map completely — which is fine, because the person holding the phone is not the one driving.

  • Rows are big and the labels are short. This gets tapped in a moving car, at a glance, by someone who should mostly be watching the road.
  • Every tap gives a haptic, so it registers without looking. Different pattern for a fault and a credit.
  • The last tap stays on screen with an Undo next to it. Mis-taps in a moving car are certain, and a wrong serious fault would otherwise silently fail the test.
  • The tally reads +4 · 3 minors so the running state is legible without opening anything.

Tap MIN Minor Amber. Fifteen of these and the test is failed.

Tap plus Done well A credit. Never cancels a fault — see below.
Undo Removes the last mark Always on the collapsed bar, whatever the last mark was.

Three buttons rather than a long-press. An earlier draft had you hold the minus button to escalate a minor into a major. That is one slip away from failing someone’s test by accident, on a gesture nothing on screen advertises. Two labelled buttons cost a few millimetres and remove the whole class of mistake — and at a real row height both are comfortable thumb targets.

Correction

No category is a minor or a major

I checked DVSA's examiner guidance and the premise was wrong — mine as much as anything. There is no list of minor faults and major faults. Every category on the test report can be marked as a driving fault, a serious fault or a dangerous fault. What decides it is the incident, not the heading it sits under.

LevelDVSA's definition, verbatim
Driving fault “One which in itself is not potentially dangerous.”
Serious “Potentially dangerous or entails a breach of the law.”
Dangerous “Involving actual danger to the examiner, candidate, the general public, or property.”

Steering is the clearest example. Drifting a little wide is a driving fault; mounting the kerb is serious; mounting the kerb where someone is standing is dangerous. Same row on the sheet, three different marks. So the panel keeps MIN and MAJ on every row, which turns out to be exactly how the real sheet works — and the useful thing the app can add is telling you where the line falls for each one.

Sources: Understanding your driving test result, DT1 Annex 6, guide to assessment and marking, and the DL25 test report itself.

Manoeuvres — only when one is asked for

Hidden until tapped open, since most of a drive has none. Reverse park, forward park, pull up on the right, and controlled stop. Marked the same way: poor accuracy or observation is a driving fault; losing control, or reversing into the road without looking, is serious.

Before setting off

Eyesight check, precautions, and the show-me tell-me vehicle checks. Done while stationary, so they get their own group and can be marked before the clock matters. A failed eyesight check ends a real test on the spot, which the app should say rather than quietly scoring it.

Left out on purpose: uncoupling and recoupling, which is lorry and trailer tests only, and fuel-efficient driving, which DVSA records but explicitly does not count towards the result.

The rule worth automating

Repeating a minor turns it into a major

Straight from the examiner guidance: a candidate “who habitually commits a driving fault in one aspect of driving throughout the test cannot be regarded as competent to pass the test. The repeated fault can then be assessed as potentially dangerous.”

This is the single most misunderstood thing about the test — people count to fifteen and assume they are safe. The app already knows how many minors each category has, so it can say so: at the third minor on the same row, the row turns amber and the collapsed bar warns “Mirrors ×3 — an examiner would likely call this serious”.

It warns, it does not decide. DVSA gives no number — “habitually” is the examiner's judgement, and inventing a threshold that silently fails someone would be worse than saying nothing. So the count and the warning are shown, and it stays the person in the passenger seat's call whether to press MAJ.

Terminology

What minor and major mean here

The app currently says “minor” and “serious”. Everyone learning to drive says minors and majors, so that is what the panel and the result screen will say. Worth being exact about what each one maps to, because the pass rule hangs off it.

In the appDVSA calls itEffect
Minor Driving fault Allowed. Sixteen or more fails — so fifteen is the most you can carry.
Major Serious or dangerous fault One fails the test, immediately, whatever else happened.
Credit Nothing — the real sheet has no equivalent This app’s own idea. Affects the practice score only, never the verdict.

DVSA separates serious from dangerous — serious is potentially dangerous, dangerous means actual danger was caused. Both fail the test on the spot, and asking someone to make that call at 30 mph buys nothing, so the panel collapses them into one major. If a post-test review screen ever wants the distinction, it can be drawn there instead.

This renames things in code too. Fault.severity becomes "minor" | "major" and MockResult.serious becomes majors, so the result screen and the automatic speeding and observation faults all speak the same language as the panel. No behaviour changes — a sustained speeding fault is still the more severe kind, it is just called a major now.

Scoring

Credits must not be able to buy a pass

The real test has no positive marks. DVSA examiners record faults only: sixteen minors, or one major, and you fail. If a handful of taps on the plus side could turn a fail into a pass, the app would tell someone they were ready for a test they would not pass — which is the one thing it exists to prevent.

// Pass or fail: exactly the DVSA rule, untouched by credits passed = majors === 0 && minors <= 15 // The 0-100 practice score is this app's own idea, so credits belong here score = 100 − 5×minors − 40×majors + min(credits, 10) // ...but capped so a failed test can never look like a comfortable pass score = passed ? score : min(score, 55)

So credits are worth up to ten points on the practice score, and nothing at all on the verdict. The result screen gains a What went well section listing them, which is where they actually earn their keep — a learner reading three faults and nothing else has no idea what to keep doing.

Also recommended

Each mark remembers where it happened

A tap already knows the time and the GPS fix. Storing them costs nothing and means the result screen can say “Mirrors — minor, 4:12, near Queensway” instead of just “Mirrors”. It also makes a future map replay of the test possible without another data change.

Needs a decision later

Credits are not saved between sessions yet

Saved progress currently stores a list of fault tags and a score. Credits would need a new column to survive. For now they appear on the result screen and feed the score, but a past test reopened later shows faults only. Worth doing properly, separately.

The map, still open

One question from last time

The map port is unchanged from the previous design: Mapbox instead of Apple Maps, Waze palette and wide roads, the three-layer puck, manual heading-up camera, the two-source rotation model, and the route line going grey behind you. The shared NavMap component still gets extracted so practice and mock cannot drift apart.

Option A · recommended

Score on raw GPS, draw on snapped

The map shows the tidy snapped position; the scoring engine keeps receiving the untouched fix. Observation zones and speeding are judged on what the phone measured, so the display can never invent a fault or hide one — and this change cannot alter anyone’s score.

Option B

Score on snapped too

Steadier zone detection, but snapping assumes you are on the route. Take a wrong turn in a mock test and it would keep reporting you on the line, possibly crediting a zone you drove nowhere near.

Still worth doing first: the practice screen has not been confirmed working on your phone. Extracting its map makes it harder to tell a pre-existing bug from one the extraction caused. One drive of the practice route removes that ambiguity — or say the word and I will build straight through and you can check both at once.
