RouteReady · design · 30 August 2026

Marking without looking

Two changes to the mock test: get rid of the wait before it starts, and make recording a fault fast enough that the score keeps up with the drive. Both are about the same person — a parent in the passenger seat, phone in a cradle, eyes mostly on the road.

01 · decision one

Getting rid of “Confirming your position”

Starting a mock test currently costs two taps with an open-ended wait between them.

tapStartOn the preview card, once you are within 60 m of the start.
→
waitConfirming your positionThree accepted fixes spanning at least 3 s, within 30 m, under 5 mph. No progress bar, because nothing knows how long it takes.
→
tapReady to begin?A second card, asking the same question the first tap already answered.
→
nowThe test runsClock starts, marks become possible.
What the wait is actually protecting — and what it isn't

It is not protecting the score. A drive with no GPS reaches finish() with no samples, and the store already refuses to score it: samples.length > 0 ? result : { assessable: false, passed: false, score: 0 }. That guard is what stops a test taken indoors reading as a flawless pass, and it works whether or not this gate exists.

What the gate does buy is a promise that the clock starts on the start line rather than in the kitchen. That is worth keeping — but it can be stated instead of enforced, which is what the options below do.

Recommended A · One tap, and say what is missing

tapBegin mock testThe only gate. The card already knows you are at the start.
→

removedConfirming your position  ·  Ready to begin?
You’re on the start line
Route 1 · Watford Way 17.0 km · 26 min · loop
Begin mock test
The clock starts now. Nothing before this counts.

The card only offers this wording when you are already inside the start radius, which it can tell from the last known fix — that is the same condition that collapses it to a single “Start” button today. Further away it still offers Guide me to the start, unchanged.

Alternative B · Keep one prompt, drop the wait

tapStartOpens the begin prompt immediately, no fix required.
→
tapBegin routeThe existing prompt, reached at once instead of after a wait.
removedConfirming your position

The smallest possible change: the start prompt stops being gated on the lifecycle reaching start-prompt and opens on the tap. Two taps rather than one, but it keeps the “are you ready” beat that some people will want before an assessed drive begins.

Worth knowing: the second card currently carries the route name and length, which is the last chance to notice you picked the wrong route. Option A moves that onto the begin card itself rather than losing it.

The one thing that must not change

Whichever option, beginTest() must still be the only place the store is told anything. Nothing is marked, timed or sampled before that tap — a fault cannot be scored against a driver who has not been told the test has begun. Removing the gate shortens the road to that tap; it does not move the tap.

The drag is the new cost, and it is my doing. The sheet used to rest open on the marking grid; it now rests collapsed because at the marking height it covered the Mirrors button and the Mapbox attribution — Mirrors decides part of the score, and the attribution is a condition of the Mapbox licence. That was the right call for the driving surface and the wrong one for the passenger, and this section is the fix.

A constraint that rules one obvious answer out

The tempting fix is to put a row of quick-mark chips in the collapsed sheet. It cannot go there. The controls row is stacked off the collapsed sheet's height, and the driver puck sits at 62% of the screen — so every point added to the sheet pushes the row up into the puck:

A comment I wrote in mock.tsx claims 17.5 pt of clearance there — it was computed from an estimate of the meter height that turned out to be 18 pt short. The comment needs correcting whichever option wins, and the bottom of a 667 pt screen has no spare room at all.

So the marking control must add no height. Both options below reuse space that already exists.

The fault is already recorded and the meter has already moved. For about eight seconds the message slot offers Serious — which upgrades the mark in place — and Undo. Escalation is the exception, so it costs the extra tap rather than the common case paying for it.

This is the one real trade in the whole document: a mis-tapped tile is a recorded fault for a few seconds rather than a discarded choice. Undo is right there, the haptic fires on every mark, and the meter is visible — but if you would rather nothing be recorded until severity is chosen, that is option B.

Exactly today's two-step, reached from the Mark button instead of from a drag. Nothing is recorded until severity is chosen, and each category still shows where the line falls between a driving fault and a serious one — the wording DVSA would use, written per category in examiner.ts.

Costs one more tap on every fault than A, and the passenger has to make a judgement in the moment rather than after.

Not recommended C · Long-press for serious

Tap the tile for a driving fault, hold it for a serious one. One tap for everything, no confirmation bar, no second step.

Rejected on two counts. A long press is invisible — nothing on the tile says it exists, and this is a surface used a handful of times a month by someone who is not the app's owner. And a hold has no failure mode: a press that was a hair too short silently records the wrong severity, and the difference between a driving fault and a serious one is the difference between a pass and a fail.

That cap exists so a turned-up text size cannot push the manoeuvre banner off the screen, and it is worth revisiting on its own rather than as part of this change.

05 · boundary

What none of this touches

  • Scoring. aggregate() stays a pure function of the faults handed to it, and the live meter keeps counting exactly what the verdict counts — marks, missed observation zones and speeding.
  • The no-evidence guard. A drive with no samples stays unassessable however it was started.
  • Finish, off-route detection, the return leg and deviation recording.
  • The drive to the start, and the preview card when you are far from it.
  • The practice screen, which shares the preview card and start prompt. Option A changes wording only when the caller is the mock test, so practice is untouched.
