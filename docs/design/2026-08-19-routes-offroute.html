Back on Route

Rebuilding the practice loops to the length of a real test, and guiding a learner back to the line when they leave it. One finding up front changes the shape of the job.

01The finding

Real DVSA routes cannot be sourced for these centres

You asked me to scan the internet for past DVSA routes. I did. Here is what is actually out there.

The sites selling “2026 DVSA routes” as GPX sell reconstructed crowd traces as a licensed product. Scraping those into a shipped app is infringement.

So the literal request is not buildable

There is no lawful source of genuine past DVSA routes for Mill Hill, Hendon, Croydon or the other 37. Your own code already knew this — the header comment in scripts/seed/routePlan.ts says so.

What we build instead — you chose this

Generate loops that are examiner-realistic by construction: built from what DVSA does publish, which is what the test contains. Same OSM data the app already fetches, no licensing exposure, and it covers all 40 centres rather than none.

02Ground truth

The loops are already loops. The lengths are the problem.

Second correction, and a happier one. I measured all 400 seeded routes: every one starts and ends at its test centre, first coordinate to last, gap 0.00 m. planRoutes already pushes the centre in as both the first and the last waypoint. That half of the request is done.

What is genuinely broken is duration. A real practical test is about 40 minutes of driving. The seed spans 7 to 72 minutes and 3.4 to 49 km — because the planner controls the radius its waypoints sit at, and never the length of the drive that results.

03Route generation

Six phases, not three bearings

Today a route is three waypoints chosen by compass bearing and feature kind. The rewrite models a route as the ordered sequence of things a test contains, each phase resolved to a real OSM feature near the centre. Length is then driven to target by expanding or contracting the middle phases until OSRM reports 36–42 minutes.

1

Settle-in leg

Quiet residential streets straight out of the centre. Every examiner starts you somewhere undemanding.

highway=residential
2

Junction work

A cluster of roundabouts and signalled junctions — the densest source of faults, so it comes early while concentration is fresh.

junction=roundabout · highway=traffic_signals
3

Faster road

Primary, trunk or dual carriageway where one exists within reach. Skipped for a rural centre that has none, rather than faked.

highway=primary|trunk · dual_carriageway=yes
4

Independent driving

The longest single stretch, chosen between two signposted destinations so it can be followed by road sign alone — about 20 minutes in a real test. The app narrates the destination, not each turn.

destination · destination:ref on the way
5

Manoeuvre pull-in

A wide, quiet residential street with on-street parking — somewhere a parallel park or a pull-up-on-the-right is actually possible. Tagged on the route so the app can prompt it.

highway=residential + parking:lane=parallel
6

Return leg

Back to the centre, length-tuned. This is the phase that absorbs the difference between the drive so far and the 40-minute target.

— routed to the centre

Two phases are optional by design. A centre with no fast road inside its band drops phase 3 rather than reaching 20 km to find one — which is precisely how the current planner produced that 49 km route.

04Off-route detection

Five states, and the hysteresis that stops them flapping

The plumbing is half-built already: navSnap in src/lib/navmap.ts returns an onRoute flag at a 40 m threshold, and nothing consumes it. A new pure module, src/lib/offRoute.ts, turns that flag into a state machine.

Why a machine rather than an if: a single fix beyond 40 m means nothing. Urban canyons, multi-storey car parks and tunnel mouths throw fixes hundreds of metres wide. Entering and leaving on different thresholds, and requiring consecutive fixes in both directions, is what stops the banner strobing at every red light beside a tall building.

05The rejoin rule

Cheapest safe rejoin, and an honest word about what got skipped

You chose sat-nav behaviour over route purity, which I think is right: on a missed roundabout exit, “return to where you left” means instructing a U-turn, and an app should not be telling a learner to do that.

ConstantValueWhy that number
OFF_ROUTE_ENTER_M45Just past the existing 40 m snap radius, so the two never disagree about the same fix.
OFF_ROUTE_EXIT_M25A 20 m gap below entry. Noise that crosses one threshold cannot cross both.
ENTER_FIXES3At 1 Hz, roughly three seconds of genuinely being somewhere else.
EXIT_FIXES2Faster to forgive than to accuse.
SKIP_WARN_M250Below this the rejoin is not worth mentioning; above it the driver is told what they missed.

07Mock test

Same help, and a result that tells the truth

You chose identical guidance in a mock test, with each departure logged. The learner still gets talked back safely — but the result screen says what happened, because a mock result that quietly hides a wandered drive is not worth taking.

One consequence worth knowing before we start

Reseeding replaces all 400 routes. push.ts truncates test_centres with cascade, so existing user_progress and favourites rows go with them. That is already true of every seed run today — but this is the first one where you have real progress to lose.

09Decided

Where this landed

Where do routes come from?
Examiner-realistic generation. Six phases from OSM, length-tuned to the real test. Not scraped GPX, not curated by hand.
Where do we send a driver who strays?
Cheapest safe rejoin, warn on skip. Never a U-turn; the skipped distance is spoken, not swallowed.
Does a mock test behave differently?
Same guidance, logged as a deviation. Safe in the car, honest on the result screen.
